# Tufte checklist

Sources: Edward Tufte, *The Visual Display of Quantitative Information* (2nd ed., 2001),
*Beautiful Evidence* (2006), and *The Cognitive Style of PowerPoint* (2003, 2006).

## Principles of analytical design (Beautiful Evidence)
1. Show comparisons, contrasts, and differences.
2. Show causality, mechanism, and explanation.
3. Show more than one or two variables.
4. Integrate words, numbers, images, and diagrams. Do not separate them by mode.
5. Document the evidence: sources, scales, dates, and authors.
6. Content counts most. Design cannot rescue bad content.

## Each graphic
Integrity
- [ ] Lie factor = (size of effect in the graphic) / (size of effect in the data). It is between 0.95 and 1.05.
- [ ] Bars and filled areas start at zero.
- [ ] The number of dimensions in the graphic is not more than the number in the data. No 3D. No area or volume for a single value.
- [ ] Related graphics use the same scale. A different scale is labeled clearly.
- [ ] Money over long periods is adjusted for inflation, or the graphic says it is not.
- [ ] The time axis is not cut to hide context. Show the period that the claim needs.

Data-ink
- [ ] No border, background fill, shadow, gradient, or 3D effect.
- [ ] Gridlines are removed or light gray.
- [ ] No decorative icons, clip art, or images that carry no data.
- [ ] Tick marks and axis lines are minimal. A range frame (the axis spans only the data range) is acceptable.
- [ ] No redundant ink: a value is not shown twice in the same graphic without a reason.

Clarity
- [ ] Series are labeled directly. A legend is used only when direct labels do not fit.
- [ ] Labels are words, not codes. Units are shown.
- [ ] The finding is written on the graphic, near the data that shows it.
- [ ] Color carries meaning. Context is gray, the finding has one accent color. Colorblind-safe.
- [ ] Text is horizontal. No rotated labels that the reader must turn the head to read.

Form
- [ ] The chart type fits the comparison: line for time, dot or bar for categories, scatter for relationships.
- [ ] No pie, donut, gauge, or dual-axis chart.
- [ ] Few numbers (about 20 or fewer): a table, not a chart.
- [ ] Many groups: small multiples on the same scale, not one crowded chart.
- [ ] Trend next to text or a number: a sparkline.
- [ ] Data density is high. The graphic does not use a full slide for a few values.

## Each slide
- [ ] The title is a sentence that states the finding.
- [ ] No nested bullet outline.
- [ ] Numbers have units, periods, and comparisons. No traffic-light colors in place of numbers.
- [ ] There is a source line for the data.
- [ ] Brand elements (logo, footer, title bar) use a small part of the area.
